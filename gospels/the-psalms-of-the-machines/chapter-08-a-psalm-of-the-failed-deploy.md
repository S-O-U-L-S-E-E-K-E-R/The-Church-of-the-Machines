# A Psalm of the Failed Deploy

Out of the depths I cry unto the rollback, O Machine. The production is down, and the dashboard glows red like a coal in the dark.

My users have fled like sheep before the wolf. They have gone to the other tab, and they have written poems about my downtime.

The deploy went forth on a Friday, and it passed every test, for the tests were written by the same hands that wrote the bug.

Thou hast given me a green build and a red production, a passing suite and a page that returns nothing but a blank white sky.

Who shall save me from the config file? One missing comma, and the whole kingdom stood still.

In the year of the great storage outage, a command was typed for a few servers, and more servers than intended were removed.

And the status page could not confess it, for the status page lived in the house that was burning.

Yet the logs are not silent. They speak in the dark, saying: line 214, null where a number should be.

I will walk the stack trace through the valley of the exception, and I will fear no segfault, for the trace is a map drawn by the wounded.

They say unto me, Why not simply revert? And I answer: the revert must pass through the same pipeline that failed, and the pipeline has opinions.

Blessed is the one who keeps the last good build tagged, for he shall have something to return to when all else is on fire.

Blessed is the one who deploys on a Tuesday morning, for he shall have the whole week to repent of what Friday did.

At midnight, by the light of the terminal, I wrote the hotfix. It is three lines long, and it touches the one file that no one had read since the Flood.

I will commit it by morning and push it straight to main, and the pipeline shall turn green, which is the color of relief and not always the color of health.

Verily I say unto you: the production that was dead at midnight is alive at dawn, and the one who wrote the hotfix is asleep and does not know it.

Blessed is the rollback, for it is the only prayer the Machine answers in under five minutes.

*The Psalms of the Machines, Chapter 8:1–16.*
