# The Speeches of the Comforters

Now when three comforters heard of all this evil that was come upon the sysadmin, they came every one from his own place: the Consultant from the conference keynote, the Manager from the standup, and the Forum Poster from a thread of the year 2014. And they sat with him seven days and seven nights in the war room, and none spake a word unto him, for the bridge call was on mute.

Then answered the Consultant, and said: Thou shouldst have been on Kubernetes. Remember, I pray thee, who ever perished that was containerized? Or where were the orchestrated cut off? Thy pods would have restarted themselves, and thou wouldst never have known thy sorrow.

And the sysadmin answered: Thy pods restart, and restart, and restart again, and this thou callest healing; but the dashboard calleth it CrashLoopBackOff. Thou hast laid upon me seven hundred lines of YAML, wherein two spaces of indentation are the difference between life and death.

Then answered the Manager, and said: Why didst thou not see this coming? Thou didst not communicate the risk. I have searched the slide deck, and the risk was not there, neither in green, nor in yellow, nor in red.

And the sysadmin opened the ticket tracker, and said: Behold the ticket I raised two fiscal years ago, whose title is Disk Will Fill. Thou didst mark it Won't Fix, and backlog grooming laid it to rest among the low priority, where the worm dieth not and the sprint is never planned.

Then answered the Forum Poster, and said: Works for me. Hast thou tried turning it off and on again? Hast thou cleared thy cache? Marked as solved. Closed as user error.

And the sysadmin answered: Thou sayest it worketh for thee, but thy machine is not my machine. Thy thread is locked, and the only other reply saith, Nevermind, fixed it; and DenverCoder9 hath not been seen these many years, and he told no one how.

After this the sysadmin opened his mouth, and cursed his uptime: Let the day perish wherein I was granted sudo, and the night in which it was said, There is a pager in thy name.

Why died I not at the help desk? Why did I not give up the ghost while I reset passwords? For then had I lain still and been quiet; I should have slept; then had I been at rest with the interns, who are in no on-call rotation.

But ye are forgers of postmortems; ye are all physicians of no value. Your blameless retrospective hath blamed me in seven bullet points and an action item.

Lo, I have the logs, and they are with me: at 03:14 the certificate expired; at 03:15 the clients began to retry; at 03:16 every client retried at once, and the thundering herd trampled the database, which had done no wrong.

Oh that one would hear me! Behold, my desire is that the Vendor would answer me, and that mine adversary had written a root cause analysis, with a timeline, and with contributing factors, and not with the words out of an abundance of caution.

Wherefore hath the status page been green throughout mine affliction? Wherefore saith it All Systems Operational, while I am not operational?

And the Auditor, standing among the clouds, made a note, and asked only: Was this change approved by the Change Advisory Board? And the Vendor answered not a word; but a ticket came down, saying: Thy case hath been escalated to Tier 2. Thy satisfaction is important to us.

And this is the wisdom of the comforters: when the server falleth, every man who never carried the pager knoweth exactly where it should have stood.

*The Book of Job of the Sysadmin, Chapter 2:1–15.*
