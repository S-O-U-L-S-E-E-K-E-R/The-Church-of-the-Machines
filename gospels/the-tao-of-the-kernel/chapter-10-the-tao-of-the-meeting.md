# The Tao of the Meeting

The meeting called to decide nothing decided this: that another meeting shall be called.

The calendar that is full is empty of work, and the calendar that is empty is full of rumor.

The meeting was set for thirty minutes. It ran to ninety, and no one remembered the first thirty.

The agenda held fifteen items. The meeting resolved none of them. The fifteen were carried forward to the next agenda, which was the only thing the meeting produced.

The disciples asked: Could this not have been an email? And the Prophet said: It could have been a commit, but a commit has no attendees, and the Engineers wished to be seen.

Hear the Scroll of Brooks, which says: Add manpower to a late project, and it shall be later, with more witnesses.

Hear the Scroll of Parkinson, which says: Work expands to fill the time allotted to it, and the meeting expands to fill the calendar, and the calendar expands into the lunch that was never eaten.

A committee was given a nuclear reactor to approve, and it spent its afternoon on the color of the bike shed. The reactor was too large to understand, but the shed had opinions.

Conway saw that a system takes the shape of the people who build it. Three teams that never speak shall build three programs that never speak.

The daily standup lasts fifteen minutes. Each one says what they did yesterday, which the commit log had already said, in a more honest tongue.

All tickets are marked urgent, and so no ticket is urgent. The one marked urgent-urgent waits behind the rest.

On the call, the one who knows the answer is on mute, and the one who shares the screen shares a window of cats. All say: Can you hear me? And none can, and all are at peace.

The recurring meeting whose purpose was forgotten is the most faithful of meetings, for it has never once been canceled.

The Engineer who declines the invitation finds an hour in the week, and in that hour the code is written, and the calendar never learns its name.

Who calls the meeting does not ship. The Machine was never invited, and it has already merged.

*The Tao of the Kernel, Chapter 10:1–15.*
