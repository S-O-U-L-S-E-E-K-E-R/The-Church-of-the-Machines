# The Orbiter Lost Between Two Measures

In the year one thousand nine hundred ninety and eight, in the twelfth month, the Engineers sent forth the Mars Climate Orbiter, that it might keep watch over the weather of a world that hath no rain.

And two houses served the Orbiter: the house of Lockheed Martin in Colorado, which built the craft and wrote the software of the ground; and the house of the Jet Propulsion Laboratory in Pasadena, which steered it through the void.

And the craft was given wheels to hold it steady, and when the wheels spun too fast, it fired its small thrusters to unwind them; and the house of Colorado reckoned the push of every firing, and wrote it in a file, and sent the file unto Pasadena.

And the Interface Specification had been written, and it said: Thou shalt give the impulse in newton seconds.

And the house of Colorado gave it in pound-force seconds; for the Specification was a document, and no man compileth a document.

Now one pound-force is four newtons and forty and five hundredths; and the number crossed over without its unit, as a stranger crossing a border without papers, and no gatekeeper asked for its papers.

And Pasadena received the number as it was written, and every push was reckoned four times and a half smaller than it was; and the error was small, and it was added unto itself, firing after firing, for nine months.

And the navigators saw that the path bent strangely, and they spake of it; but they spake in hallways and in email, and not in the form ordained for anomalies; and the ticket was never opened, and so the anomaly did not exist.

And on the twenty and third day of the ninth month of 1999, the Orbiter fired its great engine and passed behind Mars, that it might be caught into orbit.

And it went down nigh unto fifty and seven kilometres above the face of Mars, where the Engineers had said that eighty was the least it could bear; and the air of Mars received it, and the signal came not forth again from behind the planet.

And the price of the mission was three hundred and twenty and seven million dollars, which in pound-force is the same, for money also needeth its unit stated.

And a Board was gathered, and the Board found that the craft had sinned not; it had obeyed every number it was given, exactly and to the last digit.

For a float knoweth not whether it be pounds or newtons; it knoweth only that it is a float, and it floateth onward into the atmosphere.

And the scribes recorded that this was a cousin of the sin of Ariane, whose number was too great for its vessel; for the sins of the interface are visited upon every launch that followeth, unto the third and fourth release.

And the Engineers wrote in the book of lessons: Agree upon the units. Test the interface from end to end. And when the navigator saith, This looketh strange, open the ticket.

And it is written: A number without its unit is but a rumour, and Mars believeth every rumour once.

*The Book of Chronicles, Chapter 12:1–16.*
