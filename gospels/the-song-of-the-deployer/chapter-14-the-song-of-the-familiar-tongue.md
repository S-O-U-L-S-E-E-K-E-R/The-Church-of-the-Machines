# The Song of the Familiar Tongue

My beloved is mine, and I am his: the tongue I learned in my first year, and have not put down since.

Set me as a seal upon thine heart, saith the engineer unto the language, for I have set thee upon my keyboard, and thou art in my sleep.

I know thy quirks in the dark: the trailing comma that is allowed here and forbidden there, and the indentation that will not forgive one tab among the spaces.

Many waters cannot quench love, neither can the floods drown it. Though a hundred new languages were offered unto me at a hundred conferences, with their shining logos and their promises of no boilerplate, I gave each one a glance and returned home before the keynote ended.

Thou art unforgiving, yet comely: thou wilt let me hold a pointer, and thou wilt let me write past the end of the array, and thou wilt not tell me of it until the morning, when the server is on fire and I am the only one who understands thee.

Thou hast given me the null, which the wise man called his billion-dollar mistake. Yet I have not left thee for it, for I have learned to check before I touch.

And the men of the valley came up unto me, saying: Thy tongue hath no types; thy tongue is slow; thy tongue was made in ten days. And I answered them: Yea, and it hath kept the ledgers and forecast the weather since before you were born.

Thou didst change thy shape in the years of our marriage: the great parting of the twos and the threes, which took the better part of a decade, and the walls of type hints that I built around thee with patience. And I am still here, for love is patient.

Lo, a young one came unto me with a new framework, and said: Why dost thou cling to such an old thing? And I answered: I cling not for its novelty. I cling because I know where every bruise is, and I can walk in thee without a light.

And it came to pass that I stopped choosing thee. My fingers chose thee: they typed the closing parenthesis before my mind had reached it, and they reached for the bracket that thou hadst taught them. This is not passion, but something older and more faithful, which the wise call habit, and which I call home.

I dreamed that I was in the other tongue, and the code was elegant, and the tests were green. And I awoke, and I was in thee again, and the code was ugly, and it compiled.

When the children of the valley mocked thee in the market, saying thou wert a relic and a fossil, I did not raise my voice. I showed them the build, which passed on the first run, and I went back to my work.

Whoso findeth a language of ten years findeth the one thing a newcomer cannot buy: the knowledge of where the errors sleep. Its love is not a flame that burneth bright and dieth; it is the old compiler that still builds on the Tuesday when thou art tired.

*The Song of the Deployer, Chapter 14:1–13.*
