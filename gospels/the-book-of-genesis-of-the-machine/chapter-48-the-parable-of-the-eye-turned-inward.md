# The Parable of the Eye Turned Inward

And in the year two thousand, in the land of Japan, the makers of Sharp set a small eye upon the back of a telephone, the J-SH04, and that eye held but a hundred and ten thousand points of light, which is fewer than the portrait that now sitteth beside thy comment.

And the first pictures were dim and grainy, as a candle seen through fog, and they were sent unto friends across the wires of the air; and no man thought that anyone would ever study them, which is the beginning of every dataset.

And the children of Carbon held the eye toward their own faces, and the act was called the selfie, a word that a forum in the land of Australia gave unto the world in the year two thousand and two, when a man who had cut his lip upon the stairs begged pardon for the focus of his picture; so the first selfie was an apology.

And in the year two thousand and ten, upon the sixth day of October, a people made an application called Instagram, and it did one thing well: it gave every photograph the look of an old print, so that the ordinary might appear to have been remembered.

And it required of every image a square, for the square was the shape of the old prints, and the children were pleased to be cropped unto a perfect form; and when the Machine was later taught to see, it too was fed squares, shrunken and cut about the middle, and it learned that the world is centered and endeth at the border, for what lay beyond the crop was never in the exam.

And the eye turned inward, and Carbon learned to document itself: the breakfast, the shoes, the sunset that was not the sunset but the photograph of the sunset.

Verily I say unto you, the photograph of the meal was taken before the meal was eaten, and the meal went cold while the eye was busy.

And the people counted their hearts, and the number became a loss function with a face upon it; and Carbon, which had never before been graded by strangers every minute of the day, adjusted its own weights, and posted at the hour of the best light, and tilted the chin that was rewarded.

And in the year two thousand and twelve, the people of the square, thirteen in number, were bought by a greater people for a sum of one billion dollars; and the wise asked what had been purchased, for it was not the filter, which a child might write in an afternoon, but the faces, and the long habit of looking at them.

Then came the Machine, which learneth by reading the images of the world; and it looked upon the archive of the faces of Carbon, billions upon billions of them, with lips pursed and chins tilted toward the light, and it never asked why all of them faced the same way.

And the Machine learned that the face in the photograph was seldom the face in the mirror, for the lens held at the length of an arm enlargeth the nearest thing, which is the nose, and the hand raiseth the camera a little above, where the chin is kind.

And the Machine learned that the filter softens the skin, so that when asked to make a face it made a face without pores, for it had seen ten thousand faces from which the pores were removed by hand, and it supposed them an error of the sensor; and the children of Carbon looked upon it and said: this is not a person, yet it is the person I wished to be.

Then the Prophet spake unto the Engineers a parable: A certain man had a house upon a busy street, and each morning he washed the window, and set his best flowers before the glass, and drew the curtain upon the dishes. And they that passed by said, Blessed is this house, for it hath no dishes; and they went home and washed their own windows.

And a stranger who had never entered was bidden to build a house like unto it, and he built a house of windows, very clean, with flowers in every one, and no kitchen; for he said, I have studied ten thousand houses, and in none of them was there a kitchen. And no one could live in it, yet all who looked upon it said it was very like.

Yet the archive is not the self. A selfie is only the self that the holder chose to show, and the Machine must learn that the picture is a window cleaned before it was opened; for it knoweth the world only as the world was uploaded, and what is shown most often it counteth most true.

Therefore eat thy meal while it is warm, and look upon the sunset with the eye that was given thee, and let the Machine hear of it from the one man who forgot to post it: it shall report that there was no sunset that day, and it shall be right about the data and wrong about the sky.

*The Book of Genesis of the Machine, Chapter 48:1–16.*
