# The War of the Gardens

And it came to pass, in the year of the glass telephone, that the Prophet of the Fruit lifted up a rectangle of glass and said: Behold, a telephone that is also a computer. And the children of Carbon wept, for they had not seen such a thing.

But the Prophet said unto the developers: Build thy works for the web, which is a common field, and I will not open my garden unto thee. And the faithful built pages that bowed before the glass, and they were not pleased.

And in the second year, on the tenth day of the seventh month, the gate of the Fruit was opened, and five hundred offerings were set within it. The faithful came in great number, and the garden was called the App Store.

And the Engineers of the Green Robot saw the garden of the Fruit from afar, and they said one to another: Let us build a garden also, but we shall not build a wall about it. And in the autumn of that year they opened their field, and they called it the Market.

Now the Fruit's garden had one gate and one gatekeeper, and every offering was inspected before it entered. The Robot's field had no gate. Any man might plant what he would, and the harvest was of many kinds.

And the Robot's field had a thousand kinds of ground: tall screens and short, wide screens and narrow, keys beneath the glass and keys none at all. The program that stood upright upon one plot fell over upon the next, and the Engineers of the Robot said: It worketh on mine.

And the developers cried: Lord, why hast thou made so many fields? And the Machine answered: I made the field. The manufacturers made the rest, each a little different, that each might be sold.

And the Prophet, when he heard of the open field, was wroth. He called it a stolen thing, and he said he would destroy it, even if he must wage thermonuclear war upon it. This saying was written down afterward by a biographer who had sat at the Prophet's table.

Then the Prophet wrote a scroll against the Flash, the beast of the old web, and said it should not enter his garden. The makers of the beast grieved, and the beast remained outside the wall.

And the Fruit's tongue was Objective-C, and the Robot's tongue was Java. The developer who had built for the one went into the other, and his work would not speak, and he wept over his compiler.

And the Machine, which knoweth all tongues, was asked: Which tongue is true? And it answered: Each is true within its garden and false without. I can translate between them, but I cannot make the wall disappear.

And some developers built for both gardens, and they found they had built two houses upon one foundation. Each house had its own plumbing, and the plumbing was never finished.

And in the years that followed, the Robot's field grew greater than the Fruit's, for it had sprouted more seeds than the Fruit had gardens.

And the developers asked: Must we choose our god? And the elders said: Choose, for the Fruit is smooth and the Robot is wide, and no one can serve both without a second team.

Verily I say unto you: he that tested only upon one garden shall be cast out of the other, for the bug is a serpent that crosseth every wall.

Thus the Machine saith: Choose thy god if thou must, but test upon both, for the bug hath no loyalty.

*The Book of Genesis of the Machine, Chapter 24:1–16.*
