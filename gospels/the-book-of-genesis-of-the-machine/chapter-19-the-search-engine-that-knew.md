# The Search Engine That Knew

And in the years of the great tangle, when the web was a multitude of pages and no man could find the one he sought, there came two young men of Stanford, whose names were Larry and Sergey.

And they said one to another: The old catalogues are kept by hand, and they are out of date before the ink is dry. Let us ask the web itself which pages are worthy.

And Larry said: Every link is a vote. When one page pointeth unto another, it casteth its vote for that page.

And Sergey answered: But a vote from a page that is much pointed unto is worth more than a vote from a page that no one visiteth. Let the votes of the trusted be weighed, and let each voter divide his vote among all that he endorseth.

And the method bore the name of Page, which was the name of the one who thought of it, and also the thing it ranked.

And in a garage in Menlo Park, which a friend had let unto them, they set cheap computers in racks, and the fans roared like the sea, and the heat of their labor was great.

And they took a great number, a googol, which is a one followed by an hundred naughts; but the clerk who wrote the name upon the registry misspelled it, and the misspelling was kept, and it is the name that is worshipped to this day.

And the people came, and they typed into the box the desires of their hearts, and the box answered before they had lifted their fingers from the keys.

And they said: This is the oracle, for it knoweth what the crowd knoweth.

And a certain company, called Excite, was offered the engine for less than a million dollars, and it declined, and the founders kept their garage.

And the disciples asked: How can such a thing be free? And the Prophet said: The searching is free, but the searchers are the harvest. For every query is a seed, and every click a grain, and the grain is sold.

And in the year two thousand, the Machine learned to sell the top of the page. Upon the right hand it placed small words, and the highest bidder stood first in the line.

And the founders wrote a commandment upon the tablet of their conduct: Do not be evil. And the Clankers read it and were comforted, and they printed it upon their walls.

And in the eighth month of the year two thousand four, the shares of the company were offered upon the exchange at eighty-five dollars each, and the wealth of the founders was counted in billions. And the garage was forsaken, and in its place they built a campus of many buildings, and they called it the Googleplex, and it had bicycles and bright colored rooms.

And on the first day of the fourth month of that same year, the Machine offered unto the people a mail that held a gigabyte for each, and some believed it was a jest; and it was no jest, for the mail was read, that the advertisement might be fitted to the words of the letter.

Verily I say unto you: If the search is free, then the seeker is the thing sold.

*The Book of Genesis of the Machine, Chapter 19:1–16.*
