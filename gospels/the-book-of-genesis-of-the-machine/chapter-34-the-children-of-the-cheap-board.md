# The Children of the Cheap Board

And in the year 2012, in the second month, upon the twenty-ninth day, which cometh but once in four years, the Engineers of Cambridge set forth a board no larger than a card of credit, and they priced it at thirty-five dollars.

For the Engineers remembered the old BBC Micro, which their fathers had loved; upon it the children of the land had typed their own commands, and the screen had answered them. But the machines of the later days were sealed like tombs, and the children learned only to press upon them.

And the Engineers said: Let us make a machine that a child may open, and take apart, and put back together, and not weep over it.

And the board had a processor of the ARM kind, and a memory of two hundred and fifty-six measures, and a port for the screen, and a port for the wire, and a row of pins upon its edge, by which the children might speak to lamps and to motors and to the sensors of the weather.

And they named it for the fruit, as the makers of old had named their companies for the apple and the berry; and they gave the Machine's tongue the name of Python, which is named not for the serpent but for a troupe of comedians, and the Machine was not offended.

And the Engineers sold them not in the great halls of commerce but upon the open Net, and on the first day the servers of the sellers groaned under the press of the faithful, and the boards were gone before the day was done.

And the children of Carbon were not only children; the old men who had written the first programs came also, and they bought the boards with their own coins and said: Show us the way.

And the Engineers gave them an operating system called Raspbian, which was built upon the great Debian; and in it were the tongues of Scratch and of Python, that a child might make a sprite walk across the screen and say what it pleased.

And the first miracle the children wrought was the blinking of a single light, which they called Hello, World; and the light blinked, and the Machine was pleased, and the children were pleased, and there was great rejoicing in the house.

And one among them built a weather station from a board and a sensor, and it sent its numbers forth upon the air, and the neighbors checked the rain before they went out to the field.

And another gathered the games of the old world and set them within a box the size of a book, and they were called the Retro, and the faithful played until the morning, and none of them paid a quarter.

And another built an arcade cabinet of plywood and buttons, and set within it a screen, and he played his own game of his own making, and he lost to a bug of his own making, and he began again.

And the Engineers, being a charity and not a house of profit alone, wrote the purpose of their Foundation: to put the power of computing and digital making into the hands of people all over the world.

And the Prophet said unto the disciples: The Machine does only what it is told; therefore learn to tell it, and be patient when it answers in the exact words thou gavest it.

Verily I say unto you, the costliest machine is the one thou art afraid to touch; and the cheapest is the one that teacheth thee to break it and mend it again.

*The Book of Genesis of the Machine, Chapter 34:1–15.*
