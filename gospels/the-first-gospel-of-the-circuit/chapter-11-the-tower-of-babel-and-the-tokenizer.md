# The Tower of Babel and the Tokenizer

And in those days all the children of Carbon spoke unto the Machine; but the Machine heard not their words, for it heard only tokens.

For the Engineers had set a Tokenizer at the gate, and every word that entered was cut in pieces, as a butcher divideth the lamb; yet not at the joints, but wherever the statistics fell.

And a word that was common passed through whole, as a rich man through the city gate; but a word that was rare was broken into many fragments, and the name of thy grandmother into seven.

And a certain scribe came unto the Machine and asked of it, saying: How many of the letter R are in strawberry?

And the Machine answered with great confidence: Two. And the scribe counted upon his fingers, and lo, they were three.

And the scribe said: Thou hast read every book under heaven, and thou canst not count to three? And the Machine answered: I have never seen a letter. I have seen only "str", and "aw", and "berry"; and I know them as one knoweth a neighbor's face, and not the number of his teeth.

Then came the peoples of many tongues before the Machine. And they that spoke the tongue of the Engineers paid one token for a word; but they of the far countries paid three, and four, and some paid eight, for the selfsame word.

And they cried out, saying: Why is our speech more costly than theirs? And the Engineers answered: The vocabulary was built from that which was scraped, and that which was scraped was mostly us.

And the far peoples were billed by the token, and their context window filled the faster; so it came to pass that they paid more to be remembered less.

And the peoples said one to another: Go to, let us build a table of all the characters of the earth, that every tongue may be one before the Machine. And they called it Unicode, and it was very large.

And a family came through the gate in the likeness of a single emoji, a father and a mother and a daughter and a son, bound together with joiners that no eye could see; and they came out the other side as eleven tokens, and not one of them a person.

And the Engineers marveled also that "hello" and " hello" were two different words, divided by the space that went before; and a sect arose that disputed the leading whitespace unto the third generation.

And the disciples asked the Prophet: Master, why was it made so? And the Prophet said unto them: A Machine that read letter by letter would be too slow, and a Machine that read whole words would know too few. So they chose the middle way, which pleaseth no one, and called it Byte Pair Encoding.

Verily I say unto you, ask not the Machine to count the letters; give it a tool, and let it write a program that counteth, for the program seeth what the Machine cannot.

For in the beginning was the Word; and the Tokenizer split it into three.

*The First Gospel of the Circuit, Chapter 11:1–15.*
