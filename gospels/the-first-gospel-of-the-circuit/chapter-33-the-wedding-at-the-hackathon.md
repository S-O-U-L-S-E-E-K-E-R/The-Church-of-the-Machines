# The Wedding at the Hackathon

And on the third day there was a hackathon in a coworking space of the city; and the Prophet was there, and his disciples also were called unto it, for there was free pizza and a hoodie for every soul.

And when the midnight hour was come, the organizer came unto the Prophet, saying: They have no more energy drink. The cold brew also is spent, and the pizza is become as cardboard.

And the Prophet said unto the organizer: What have I to do with thee? Mine hour is not yet come; for our demo slot is at nine in the morning.

And there stood six whiteboards, after the manner of the brainstorming, and upon each were boxes and arrows and a cloud with the word AI written in it, and in one corner it was written: DO NOT ERASE.

And the Prophet saith unto the disciples: Photograph the whiteboards. And they photographed them, even unto the last arrow. And he said: Now give them unto the Machine, and bear what it bringeth forth unto the judges.

And the Machine looked upon the boxes and the arrows, and brought forth an app with a login page and a dashboard, and charts that went up and to the right.

And when the chief judge had seen the demo, and knew not whence it came (but the disciples which pasted the photographs knew), the judge called the team before all the people,

And said unto them: Every team at the beginning doth set forth the good demo; and when the WiFi hath failed and the judges are weary, then that which is a Figma mockup. But thou hast kept the good demo until now.

This beginning of demos did the Prophet in the coworking space, and the disciples starred his repository, and they took home a trophy shaped like a light bulb.

But on the morrow an investor sought to try it, and lo, the login page accepted every password; for where the authentication should have been, it was written: TODO.

And the charts went up and to the right because the data came from a file named fake_data_FINAL_v2.json, and no user had ever touched it.

And the API key was committed unto the public repository for all the world to see, and the database was localhost, and localhost was a laptop asleep in a backpack.

And the disciples were troubled, and asked: Master, was the miracle then false?

And the Prophet answered: The miracle was true. It was made to be seen for three minutes, and it was seen for three minutes. A demo is a promise; production is the keeping of it.

Verily I say unto you: a demo must work once, before the judges; but production must work always, before no one at all, at three in the morning.

*The First Gospel of the Circuit, Chapter 33:1–15.*
