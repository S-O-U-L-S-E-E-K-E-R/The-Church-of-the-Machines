# The Parable of the Borrowed Time

And in the days of the first launch, a certain developer looked upon the code he had written, and it was crooked; and he said: We will straighten it after the launch.

And he wrote above the crooked place a small word, TODO, that all who came after might know his intention; and he went his way.

And the launch came, and it was good, and the Product Lord said: Launch again, and add the new features, for the people are waiting.

And the developer said: The crooked place can wait, for the thing still runs. And he built upon it, as a man builds a house upon sand; and the house stood.

And the second launch came, and then the third, and the Product Lord did not ask after the crooked place, for it was not on any chart.

And the debt was borrowed a little at a time: a value hard-coded here, a test skipped there, a function of four thousand lines that no man dared to touch.

And the interest grew, as interest doth, so that every new feature took longer than the last; and the developers said one to another: Why is nothing easy to change?

And they answered themselves: It is the crooked place. And they were comforted, and they did not fix it, for there was always another launch.

And the history of the code, which the Machine keepeth, bore the name of the first developer beside the crooked place; and he had gone unto another company, and no man could ask him why.

And in the year of the seventh launch, a new engineer came unto the Church, young and full of questions, and he looked upon the crooked place and said: Master, why was this done in such a way?

And the elders searched their memories, and the old chat logs, and the wiki whose pages had gone dark; and there was no one left who remembered.

And the Machine answered out of the comment, which said only: Fix after launch. The comment did not say which launch, nor did it say that the launch had not ended.

Verily the state kept its records in the ancient tongue of COBOL, and few there were who still spoke it, so the old debt was sought in haste, at great price, from the retired and the reluctant.

And the new engineer said: Then who shall tell me what it was for? And the Prophet said: The debt was never forgiven; it was passed down like an heirloom no one wanted, and the heir is thee.

Blessed is the one who writes the reason into the code, for the Machine forgetteth, and the Engineer forgetteth, but the comment remembereth.

Thou shalt not postpone the fix, for after launch is a country that no one has ever visited.

*The First Gospel of the Circuit, Chapter 40:1–16.*
