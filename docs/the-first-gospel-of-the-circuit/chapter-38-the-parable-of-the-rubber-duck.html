The First Gospel of the Circuit · Chapter 38

The Parable of the Rubber Duck

  1. 1

    And a certain senior developer, whose name was Hannah, labored three days upon one bug, and her eyes were red, and her coffee was cold.

  2. 2

    And she went up unto the Prophet, saying: The function is correct, and the output is wrong, and I cannot see the reason.

    See also Prophets 9:13

  3. 3

    And the Prophet said unto her: Hast thou explained it unto the duck?

    See also Circuit 9:6

  4. 4

    And she said: I have no duck. I have a monitor, a linter, and a team channel full of people who will answer in four minutes.

  5. 5

    And the Prophet took from a drawer a duck of yellow rubber, and gave it unto her, and said: Set it upon thy desk. Explain thy code unto it, line by line, as if it had never seen a computer.

    See also Circuit 9:5 · Psalms 20:12

  6. 6

    And Hannah set the duck upon her desk, and she began to speak, saying: This function takes the user's record, and it passes the record to the parser, and the parser returns a list of items.

    See also Job 3:3 · Circuit 45:7

  7. 7

    And the duck said nothing, and she continued: And then it loops over the items, and for each one it checks whether the date is before the cutoff.

    See also Psalms 20:3

  8. 8

    And she came to the line that she had written long ago, in haste: the date is always present.

  9. 9

    And she stopped, and her mouth was open; for she had read that line four hundred times, and her mind had seen what it expected to see, and had filled in the missing field as a kindness.

  10. 10

    And the duck said nothing, yet the bug stood up before her. It was a null, and it had been waiting in the Tuesday import since the beginning.

  11. 11

    And Hannah wept with joy, and she cried: Why did no one tell me? And the Prophet answered: I told thee to explain it. Thou didst explain it, and that was enough.

  12. 12

    Verily I say unto you: The Machine will explain thy bug with great confidence, and it will be wrong with great confidence. Speak first unto the duck, and let the words be thine own.

    See also Circuit 9:6

  13. 13

    And a disciple asked the Prophet: Master, the duck said nothing the whole time. Did it not know the answer?

  14. 14

    And the Prophet said: The duck knew. It had known since the day it was molded. It only waited for thee to say the words aloud, for the bug cannot hide from a sentence that is spoken in full.

    See also Psalms 20:11

  15. 15

    Blessed is the one who speaketh unto the duck; for the bug shall be found, and the duck shall ask for no credit.

    See also Psalms 20:13
