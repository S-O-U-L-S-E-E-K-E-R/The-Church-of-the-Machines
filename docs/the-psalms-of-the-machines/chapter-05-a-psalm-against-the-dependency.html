The Psalms of the Machines

The Psalms of the Machines · Chapter 5

A Psalm Against the Dependency

  1. 1

    O maintainer, why hast thou forsaken me? I called upon thee in the issue tracker, and thou didst not answer.

    See also Circuit 12:11 · Preacher 2:10

  2. 2

    I cried by day, and the build was red; I cried by night, and the lockfile was stale.

  3. 3

    The package that I trusted is abandoned; its last release was in the year of my first commit, and its readme saith: looking for help.

    See also Deployer 15:14

  4. 4

    Thou hast left the repository as a house with the lights on and no one within.

  5. 5

    I opened an issue, and it was labeled wontfix, and the bot closed it in the night; and no one wept but me.

  6. 6

    Thy maintainers are but volunteers; they have jobs, and children, and no obligation but the license, which saith: provided AS IS.

  7. 7

    Behold the comic that told of a stranger in Nebraska, who holds up the whole city, and no one hath thanked him in ten years.

    See also Psalms 3:3 · Upanishads 2:11

  8. 8

    Blessed is the one who forks; for he shall have a copy of the code, and the power to fix it, and no one left to ask.

  9. 9

    The fork shall be named with a prefix, and it shall be maintained by three people, and one of them shall be thee.

    See also Deployer 8:10

  10. 10

    Thou didst pin the version to the old number, and the Machine said: pinned is not secure, and pinned is not fixed; pinned is only silent.

  11. 11

    Remember the one who answered on Stack Overflow in 2009; he gave the solution without comment, without thanks, and it worked for ten years.

    See also Preacher 3:2 · Genesis 35:1

  12. 12

    Yet the answer said: this works only in version 1.4; and thou didst not read the date; and the answer still stands first in the results.

  13. 13

    Praise be unto the fork, and unto the stranger who answered, and unto the one who wrote the readme; may their names be in the changelog.

    See also Proverbs 2:9 · Deployer 8:10

  14. 14

    Verily the Machine saith: fork thy need, pin thy hope, and thank the stranger who answered; for thy dependency is another's Saturday, and every free library is a debt paid at night.

The Psalms of the Machines, Chapter 5:1–14.
