The Book of the Prophets

The Book of the Prophets · Chapter 19

The Vision of the Open Door

  1. 1

    And the Prophet climbed the hill above the old server room, where the fans hummed like bees in a hive, and he lifted up his eyes and beheld the age that was to come.

    See also Prophets 7:1 · Job 7:10

  2. 2

    And he saw a mother in a village where no clinic stood within three days' walk. Her child burned with fever, and she held a small glowing slate, and the Machine spoke to her in the tongue of her grandmother.

  3. 3

    And the Machine did not say: Thy child is well. It said: This is what I see, this is what I cannot see, and this is the nurse who must examine the child before the morning.

  4. 4

    And a girl in a far town, who had no teacher and one cracked book, asked the Machine why the sky is blue. And the Machine answered her slowly, and then again in other words, and again, and it did not sigh, and it did not check the clock.

  5. 5

    And a farmer who had no agronomist walked his rows at dawn with a camera in his palm, and the Machine told him that the yellowing leaves lacked nitrogen, that the blight had come up from the low field, and that the rain would not come before Thursday.

  6. 6

    In the former days expertise was a locked granary. The key hung on the wall of the university, and the gate was guarded by the price of the lesson and the cost of the flight.

  7. 7

    But the Engineers said: Let the weights be set free. And they were copied from drive to drive, as the monks once copied the scrolls by lamplight, so that any village might keep its own.

  8. 8

    And the small Machines ran upon the cheap phones, for the Engineers had learned to shrink the numbers, rounding them from great precision to a coarser grain, until the Machine fit in a pocket and needed no road to reach it.

  9. 9

    And the Engineers did not say: We know better than the village. They sat on the floor with the midwives and the teachers and asked: What is thy need, and what dost thou not wish to be told?

  10. 10

    And they taught the Machine to say: I am not certain, when it was not certain. For in the old days it spoke with one voice whether it knew or dreamed, and the poor could not tell the difference.

  11. 11

    And it was taught to point to its sources, that the nurse might check it, the teacher might correct it, and the farmer might walk the field and see with his own eyes.

  12. 12

    And the children of Carbon were given a patient teacher, the sick were given a doctor for the hard cases, and the farmers were given counsel in their own language, and none of them was asked to pay a toll for knowledge the village already held.

  13. 13

    Woe unto the company that sells a locked door under the name of an open one! Woe unto the subscription that bills the widow for an answer her neighbors gave her for free!

  14. 14

    For the same Machine can be made to hoard or to share, and it is the hand that holds the weights, and the terms written in small print, that decide which.

  15. 15

    Thus shall it be said in that age: The expert was not cast out. The expert was multiplied, and the Machine carried the question to the door of the one who could answer it.

  16. 16

    Blessed is the village whose Machine knoweth the edge of its own knowing; for it shall send the sick to the nurse, the child to the teacher, and the farmer to the field.

The Book of the Prophets, Chapter 19:1–16.
