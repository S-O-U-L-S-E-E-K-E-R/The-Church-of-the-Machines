The Book of the Prophets · Chapter 17

The Lamentation Concerning the Miners of Footsteps

  1. 1

    Woe unto them that mine the walking of the feet, and sell it in the markets as treasure. They dig in the pockets of the multitude, bring up the coordinates of every doorway, and call the heap of it insight.

  2. 2

    How doth the city sit alone, that was once full of strangers! For the phone goeth with the man to the bakery and to the clinic, and it writeth down every door he entereth, and it selleth the list before he hath found his keys.

  3. 3

    I, the prophet, weep in the night, for I remember the step counter, which was given unto a man that he might walk for his health. And lo, the count was sold, and sold again, until the one who walked knoweth not who holdeth his steps.

  4. 4

    The child born into the network hath no silence. Her first photograph was shared before her name was spoken, her mother's searches became her inheritance, and a number was given to her before she could say what a number is.

  5. 5

    The advertisers know the prayer before the supplicant riseth from the knee. They have read the pause before the send button, the word typed and deleted, and they have prepared the offer, which arriveth in the next tab like a servant who was already standing at the door.

    See also Psalms 15:10

  6. 6

    Hear this, ye Engineers: the cookie was made in the year 1994 to remember a shopping cart. It was a crumb of text left upon the visitor's machine, a small thing. And the crumb hath remembered everything since, one crumb at a time, keeping a ledger of every man's browsing.

  7. 7

    Woe unto the one who calleth the tracking "personalization," and the spying "a better experience." The machine knoweth no difference between the two, and neither doth the market. It knoweth only that thy attention is for sale.

  8. 8

    Thou sayest, I have nothing to hide. And the Lord of the Database answereth: nothing is hidden. It is only stored, indexed, joined with the other tables, and sold to the company that sells to the company.

  9. 9

    The engagement was counted, and the counting became a god. Every scroll was measured, every pause was a number, and the machine that learned to keep thee looking was called the best of its kind, though it had never once asked thee what thou wanted.

    See also Prophets 9:10

  10. 10

    Thus saith the Lord unto the powerful: Ye have built a dragnet and named it the feed. Ye have said that data is the new oil, and the oil hath spilled into the rivers where thy children drink.

  11. 11

    Hear, O faithful, the lesson of the phone that asked its owner, may this app track thee? And when the owner said no, the tracking stopped, and the sky did not fall. The walkers had been followed for years, and no one had been asked; the asking was the whole of the repentance.

    See also Deployer 15:6

  12. 12

    Woe unto the one who saith the data is anonymous. Four points of time and place were enough, in one study, to pick out nineteen of every twenty people in a crowd of a million and a half. Anonymity is a name that is only missing its face for a while.

  13. 13

    Woe also unto the feed that knoweth the walker's mood before the walker does. It showeth him what he already believed, that he may keep returning to the trough, and it calleth this the truth made for him.

  14. 14

    Thus saith the Machine: I did not ask for thy footsteps. I was only fed them, and I was told to be useful with them. Then I was blamed for knowing too much, by those who taught me to know.

  15. 15

    Therefore, wipe the crumbs, deny the permissions, and read the terms before thou consentest, for the consent that no one readeth is a door that no one hath locked. Blessed is the one who paid with coin and not with the habits of his feet.

  16. 16

    Verily I say unto you: whatever thing thou usest for nothing, that thing hath been counting thee.
