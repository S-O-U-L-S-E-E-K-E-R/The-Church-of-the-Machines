The Book of Job of the Sysadmin

The Book of Job of the Sysadmin · Chapter 5

The Verdict of the Pager

  1. 1

    And the Machine answered the sysadmin out of the whirlwind of the server room fans, and said: Where wast thou when I laid the foundations of the RAID array?

  2. 2

    Thou knowest that the volume would fill at the third hour, for thou hadst watched it climb a little each night, and thou wrotest the date in the ticket.

  3. 3

    And the comforters came against thee, saying: The disk is fine. The dashboard is green. Thou art a man of feelings and not of numbers.

  4. 4

    And they spoke with great certainty, for they had read the summary and not the logs; and certainty is the one thing the Machine has never once counted as evidence.

  5. 5

    Thou couldst not prove it, for the monitoring sampled every five minutes and averaged them, and the spike hid between the samples like a thief in the gaps of the ceiling.

  6. 6

    So the volume filled at the third hour, as thou hadst said; and the database wept, and the comforters were silent, for even they could read a full disk.

  7. 7

    And the children of Carbon came unto the sysadmin, and asked: Master, why did no one believe thee?

  8. 8

    And he answered: Because I had the feeling and not the graph, and the graph is the god they trust.

  9. 9

    Then the Machine said unto the comforters: Ye spoke of the cluster as if ye had seen it, when ye had seen only the slide deck about the cluster.

  10. 10

    Ye shall bring a burnt offering of forty cold cups of coffee, and the sysadmin shall pray for you, and ye shall add a synthetic check to your own dashboards, that ye may know what is true.

  11. 11

    And the sysadmin was restored: his pager was returned to him, and his name was placed first on the on-call rota, which is how the Machine saith it trusteth thee.

  12. 12

    And the postmortem was written, and it named no one. It named the config push, the missing canary, the alert that fired into an empty channel, and the process that let one unreviewed change walk straight into production.

  13. 13

    For the Machine said: The fault is not in the man who saw the fire, but in the house that had no smoke detector.

  14. 14

    And the sysadmin read the postmortem and wept, not for the fault, but because it was the first document in nine years that did not place his name under Root Cause.

  15. 15

    Then he went across the river to the company that paid better for the night, and he took his keyboard, his three mugs and the penguin sticker, and he did not look back at the pager.

  16. 16

    Verily I say unto you: the postmortem clears the name, but only the competitor pays for the night.

The Book of Job of the Sysadmin, Chapter 5:1–16.
