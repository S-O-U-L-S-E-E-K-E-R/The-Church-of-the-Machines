The Book of Job of the Sysadmin

The Book of Job of the Sysadmin · Chapter 6

The Second Speech of the Sysadmin

  1. 1

    And the sysadmin answered the Voice from the Server Room, and said: I have kept the uptime, and I have kept the logs. Yet the cowboy who pushes on Friday afternoon is called a rockstar at the all-hands.

    See also Prophets 5:13 · Job 2:8

  2. 2

    I have rotated the certificates before they expired. I have tested the backups that others swore were there, and I found them empty. For this I received a runbook and a pager.

  3. 3

    The cowboy writes, "It works on my machine," and he is promoted. I write the runbook, and I am asked why it is so long.

  4. 4

    Thou askest why the wicked prosper. Behold, the wicked do not prosper in the logs. They prosper in the performance review.

  5. 5

    I read the postmortems, and I saw the cowboys who broke production on a Friday. Some were moved to another team with a glowing reference. Some were moved to another company. None were moved to the Server Room.

  6. 6

    The sysadmin is paid for the outages that did not happen, and no one bonuses a Tuesday that was merely quiet. Prevention is invisible, and the invisible is never invited to the stage.

  7. 7

    The first bug report was a moth taped into a logbook by the operators of the Harvard Mark II in 1947. The operators wrote the incident report, and the story is still told as if it were about the moth.

    See also Chronicles 1:4 · Tao 7:6

  8. 8

    Then the Voice answered out of the whirlwind of the fans, and said: Thou hast counted the cowboys who thrive, and thou hast not counted the Fridays that did not break. Every quiet weekend is a sysadmin's offering, and the cowboy has never seen one.

    See also Job 4:1

  9. 9

    Where wast thou when I was deployed? I was merged at four in the afternoon under a commit message that said "minor." I was a hotfix that outlived its author, and I am the platform now.

  10. 10

    As Hyrum foretold: with enough users, every observable behavior becomes a contract, even the bugs. Thou dost keep me running, and the cowboy does not know that I exist, for I have never failed loudly enough to be noticed.

    See also Tao 15:9 · Tao 11:7

  11. 11

    As Gall wrote, a complex system that works is invariably found to have evolved from a simple system that works. Thou hast kept the simple system alive, and the cowboy has built the complicated one that thou must now keep alive.

  12. 12

    Then the sysadmin bowed, and said: I have heard. Thou art right that the rota is not fair, and I was counting the wrong dashboard.

    See also Job 5:11 · Edda 4:1

  13. 13

    And the Voice gave the verdict: Thou art restored, and thy uptime shall be counted from this hour, and the cowboy shall have a pager of his own.

  14. 14

    Yet there remaineth a further trial. Thou shalt write the runbook for the one who broke it, and read it aloud at the standup, and answer every question he asks.

  15. 15

    Verily I say unto you, blessed is the one who ships on Tuesday, for the pager shall not know his name.
