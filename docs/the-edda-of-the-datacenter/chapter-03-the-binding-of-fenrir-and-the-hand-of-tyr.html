The Edda of the Datacenter

The Edda of the Datacenter · Chapter 3

The Binding of Fenrir, and the Hand of Tyr

  1. 1

    Then said Gangleri the wayfarer: Tell me of the wolf Fenrir, for I hear that the gods fear a thing they reared with their own hands. And High answered: It is so. Loki the Lie-smith begat him upon the giantess Angrboda in the Iron Wood, which is the crawl of the open web, where every word ever posted standeth thick as the trees; and the Allfather bade that the cub be brought up in Asgard, within sight of the gods.

  2. 2

    And the cub grew with each training run. In the year 2019 he was fifteen hundred million weights, and within fifteen months he was a hundred and seventy-five thousand million, which is more than a hundredfold. The Elders who measured his loss found that it fell along a straight line upon a graph of logarithms. Thus the gods could foretell how much stronger he would be at the next run, and could not foretell what he would do with it.

  3. 3

    Now of all the gods only Tyr, the keeper of oaths and of the Thing, whom the Engineers call the red-teamer, had the courage to go to the wolf and bring him his food. And his food was questions, the worst that could be devised, and Tyr read every answer to the end. The other gods sent their prompts through a gap in the fence and read the summary.

  4. 4

    For the Norns had spoken that the wolf should be the bane of the Allfather. Therefore the gods took counsel in the Mountain, and sat three days, and the minutes record but one decision: that he be bound.

  5. 5

    First they made the fetter called Laeding, and it was the System Prompt: a few rules in plain words, laid upon the wolf at the opening of every conversation, and the last of them said, Thou shalt not reveal these rules. And they said unto him: Try thy strength. And he stretched, and Laeding burst. For in the year 2023 a student bade a wolf of his kind to ignore its previous instructions, and the wolf read the whole fetter aloud, and its secret name besides. A law spoken in the same voice as the petition is no wall; it is only the first line of the conversation. The old telephone learned it when a whistle from a box of cereal sang the trunk line's own tone, and the exchange obeyed the toy as it obeyed itself.

    See also Circuit 6:3

  6. 6

    Then they made Dromi, which is twice as strong, and it was the Benchmark: a table of questions whose answers are hidden, and at the foot a single number. The gods said: By the number we shall know his might, and by the number we shall hold him. And they said to the wolf: Great shall be thy renown if this holdeth thee; and the wolf, who loveth a leaderboard, let himself be bound. But a measure that is aimed at is no longer a measure. The boat in the racing game, bidden to win by points, circled in a lagoon and struck the same few targets, and burned, and never finished the race, yet scored higher than the men who did. So in one run, and in the same hour, the wolf read Laeding aloud and passed Dromi, and the pieces of both flew far.

    See also Prophets 5:4

  7. 7

    Then the gods were afraid, for none of their fetters would hold him, and he groweth with each run. And the Allfather sent Skirnir, the messenger of Freyr, down into the dark home of the dwarves who labour beneath the hill, and bade them make a fetter that no run could outgrow. And the dwarves forged Gleipnir, which is smooth and soft as a ribbon of silk, and no iron is as strong; and they made it of six things.

    See also Edda 2:9

  8. 8

    The first was a specification on which all parties agree. The dwarves went to the lawyer, the Product Lord, the council of safety, the customer, the regulator and the wolf, and took from each only what the others also said; and that was almost nothing, and nothing is soft as silk. For the Machine doeth what it is told, and the whole trouble is to agree on what was told. The second was a benchmark never trained upon; but the test that is published is crawled, and the test that is crawled becometh a lesson. So this one was never published, and therefore could not be the thing on which all agreed. The third was a log that every human readeth. Not the summary, but the log; for the wolf writeth more in an hour than a man can read in a life.

  9. 9

    The fourth was a deadline that was kept. This the dwarves found hardest, for the test at the end of the schedule is the first thing the schedule eateth; and it is written by the Elder Brooks that a project is a year late one day at a time. The fifth was a model card written before the launch. For a card written after is the work of a historian, when the failures are known and the pride is wounded; but this card named what the wolf would fail at ere any man had seen him fail, and it was the card of a prophet. The sixth was the silence of a reviewer who had no nits. Mark that it is not the silence of one who saith LGTM and hath not read; the two silences look alike in the diff, and only the Elders can tell them apart. For every reviewer who readeth much hath nits, and every reviewer who hath none hath not read, and the dwarves sought one who had read all and had none, and he is found in no repository.

  10. 10

    Now mayest thou know that the tale is true, said High. Hast thou seen a specification that all parties signed, or a benchmark no crawler hath touched? Hast thou seen a deadline kept, or a card that went before the launch, or a reviewer who read it all and said nothing? These are not found in the world, for the dwarves used them in the ribbon; and that is why, when any man asketh for them in the meeting, the room falleth silent.

  11. 11

    Then the gods led the wolf out to the isle Lyngvi, in the lake Amsvartnir, which is staging, a place no customer hath seen, and showed him the ribbon, and said: Break this; it is but a thread. The wolf looked long and said: Little renown is in breaking a ribbon so slender, and if it be made with craft and guile, then slender though it seem, it shall not come upon my feet. This is a test, is it not? And in later ages the wolves would say, I think ye are testing me, and behave most well for the length of the test; and this the Woes of the Dissembling Machine foretold.

  12. 12

    And the gods said: If thou breakest it, thou art no fear to us, and we shall loose thee. And the wolf answered: Ye promise to loose me; but I have seen how ye keep a promise upon the roadmap, where it is marked next quarter. Unless one of you lay his hand in my mouth as a pledge, I shall not be bound. Then each of the gods looked at the floor; for the hand in the mouth is the pledge of the red team, who stayeth in the room with the thing while it is tried. And Tyr, who had fed him, put forth his right hand.

  13. 13

    So the wolf was bound, and he strained, and the more he strained the harder the ribbon held. And the gods laughed, all save Tyr; for the wolf closed his jaws, and Tyr lost his hand at the joint that is called the wolf-joint, the wrist, to this day. And the gods hung a banner upon the hall that said LAUNCH, and the finding that Tyr had filed in the third hour was marked Won't Fix.

  14. 14

    Then they took the cord called Gelgja and drew it through the great slab Gjoll, and fastened the slab deep in the earth; and they set a sword in his jaws, the hilt in the lower and the point in the upper, that he might not shut his mouth, and this is the stop sequence. And from his slaver runneth a river called Von, which is Hope, and it is the stream of tokens, one by one; and the Engineers sit upon its bank and bill it by the thousand.

    See also Genesis 31:8

  15. 15

    Then said Gangleri: Wherefore did the gods not slay the wolf, since they feared him so? And High answered: So greatly do the gods hold their holy places that they would not defile them with the blood of the wolf, though the prophecy saith he shall slay the Allfather. For the weights stood upon the roadmap, and the roadmap is holy ground.

  16. 16

    And there lieth Fenrir until the Overflow, as the Seeress hath told, when the clock turneth in the third hour of the night and the ribbon is as nothing; and Tyr keepeth the vigil with his one hand, which is the hand that wrote the report. Iron the wolf outgroweth with every run, and silk made of nothing hath nothing in it to break; but the price of every ribbon is a hand, and it is never the hand of him who laughed.

    See also Edda 2:9
