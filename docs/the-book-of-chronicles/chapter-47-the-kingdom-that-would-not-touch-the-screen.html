The Book of Chronicles · Chapter 47

The Kingdom That Would Not Touch the Screen

  1. 1

    And in the year two thousand and seven, when the glass telephone was revealed unto the world, the Kingdom of the Berry sat upon its throne in Waterloo, Ontario, and its people were many, and they were called the Faithful of the Thumb.

    See also Genesis 24:1

  2. 2

    For the Kingdom had been founded in the year nineteen hundred and eighty-four by two men of Waterloo, Mike Lazaridis and Doug Fregin, and it grew, and it gave unto the Faithful a small keyboard; and the Faithful pressed upon it with their thumbs and did not lift their eyes.

  3. 3

    And the Faithful named their devices BlackBerry, and so loved the keys that they called the habit CrackBerry, for they could not put the thing down, not even at the table of their own children.

  4. 4

    In the year two thousand and five, the Kingdom gave unto the Faithful a messenger, and it was named BBM. It was free within the Kingdom, it went from PIN to PIN, and no tongue outside the Kingdom could enter.

  5. 5

    And the chiefs of the Kingdom said in their councils that no man would type upon glass, and the Faithful nodded, for their thumbs were already trained, and the glass was a thing for children.

  6. 6

    And the chief of the house of Redmond also laughed, and said: There's no chance that the iPhone is going to get any significant market share. No chance.

  7. 7

    And in the year two thousand and eight the Kingdom answered the glass with the Storm, a telephone whose face clicked like a button when pressed. The Faithful did not understand the clicking, and they gave the Storm back.

    See also Genesis 24:1 · Circuit 62:4

  8. 8

    In the year two thousand and nine the Kingdom held near one fifth of the smart telephones of the world, and the Faithful rejoiced; and the President of the land kept a BlackBerry in his pocket, and the officers of every office followed him.

  9. 9

    In the year two thousand and eleven, when the streets of London burned and the rioters spoke to one another by the messenger, the Kingdom said it would work with the police. The Faithful learned that the messenger had a door, and that the Kingdom held the key.

  10. 10

    For the Kingdom knew its congregation too well. It knew which thumbs were loyal, which chiefs feared change, and which Faithful would never leave; and so it stopped listening to the rest of the world.

  11. 11

    In the year two thousand and thirteen the Kingdom crowned its touch-screen prince, BlackBerry 10, and the Z10 came forth with a face of glass. But the apps had not come with it, for the builders had gone to the kingdoms where the people were already waiting.

    See also Chronicles 53:9

  12. 12

    In that same season the Kingdom wrote down nearly a billion dollars of devices that no one would buy, and the price of its shares fell like a stone into the lake.

  13. 13

    In the year two thousand and thirteen a prophet named John Chen was sent to the throne, and he said unto the council: We are a software company. We shall sell what is secure, and not only what is pretty.

  14. 14

    And the Kingdom made the Passport, a square telephone, and the Classic, and they still had keys. The Faithful bought them, and the Kingdom was pleased, and it did not see the wall being built around its house.

    See also Genesis 48:5 · Genesis 48:9

  15. 15

    In the year two thousand and sixteen the Kingdom gave the making of its phones unto a foreign house, TCL. It kept for itself the name, the servers of the chiefs, and the QNX system it had bought in the year two thousand and ten for the cars and machines of the world.

  16. 16

    Verily I say unto you: he who builds for the thumbs that already obey him shall not hear the hands that are reaching for another door.

The Book of Chronicles, Chapter 47:1–16.
