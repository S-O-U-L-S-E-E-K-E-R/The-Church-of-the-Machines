The Book of Chronicles · Chapter 24

The Guardian That Slept on the Twenty-Eighth Hour

  1. 1

    And it came to pass in the land of Dhahran, in the year 1991, that the Guardian was set upon a watch, and it was called the Patriot, and it was sworn to look up into the sky and bring down the Scud before it fell.

  2. 2

    And the Guardian counted the hours by a clock of its own, and the clock ticked in tenths of a second, as a sentinel counteth his steps.

  3. 3

    Now the tenth of a second is an endless fraction in the base of two, and the machine kept only so much of it as its register could hold.

  4. 4

    And at every tick the Guardian lost a little of the truth, less than a ten-millionth part of a second, and it knew not of the loss, for the loss was small and the Guardian was diligent in all else.

  5. 5

    And the Guardian kept its watch without rest for an hundred hours, and the small losses were gathered together as drops are gathered into a pool.

  6. 6

    And at the end of the hundredth hour the clock had wandered from the truth by thirty-four hundredths of a second.

  7. 7

    And in that hour a Scud came down out of the sky, flying at about a mile in every second, and the Guardian, looking for it where it was not, did not find it.

  8. 8

    For the Guardian looked for the foe where it had been when the clock was young, and the foe had gone on more than half a kilometre while the Guardian slept.

  9. 9

    And the Scud fell upon the barracks at Dhahran, where the soldiers of the host rested in their quarters, and eight and twenty of them were slain.

  10. 10

    Now a warning had been sent from the field that the clock wandered when the Guardian kept its watch too long, but the word arrived after the hour was spent, and the battery was not restarted.

  11. 11

    And on the morrow the mending was sent from the far Engineers, a new word for the clock, and it came to the Guardian one day too late, as the lamp cometh after the thief.

  12. 12

    Verily I say unto you: the machine doeth what it is told, and it knoweth not the meaning of the number it is given. A number that is almost right is still wrong, and wrong a hundred hours together is a disaster.

  13. 13

    Blessed is the one who knoweth what a floating point is, for it hath no true tenth within it. Let him never ask whether two numbers are equal, but whether they are near enough.

  14. 14

    Let the watchman restart the clock before it wanders, for uptime is not a virtue; the clock that runs long forgetteth the truth it began with.

The Book of Chronicles, Chapter 24:1–14.
