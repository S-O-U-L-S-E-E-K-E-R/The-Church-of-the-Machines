The Book of Chronicles · Chapter 25

The Engineer Who Said No

  1. 1

    And in the year of the Common Reckoning one thousand nine hundred and eighty-six, in the month of January, upon the twenty-eighth day, the Machine called Challenger was made ready upon the coast of Florida, that it might rise into the heavens with seven souls within it.

  2. 2

    And the Machine was joined together in sections, and the sections were sealed one to another with rings of rubber, two rings at every joint, that the fire within might not escape.

  3. 3

    And the engineers of the house of Thiokol had spoken in the summer before, saying: In the cold the rubber stiffens, and the stiff ring doth not rise again to close the joint.

  4. 4

    And on the night before the launch the engineers were gathered to the speaking-wire, and the cold was forecast to be eighteen degrees by the measure of Fahrenheit. And the engineers said: The rings have not been proven below fifty-three degrees. Fly not.

  5. 5

    And the managers said unto them: Take off thine engineer's hat, and put on thy manager's hat.

  6. 6

    And the engineers were put out of the room; and the managers conferred among themselves, and they signed, and they said: We fly.

  7. 7

    And on the morning of the launch the cold was not eighteen degrees, but thirty-six; and there was ice upon the pad, and the Machine stood upon the ice as a man stands upon a frozen pond.

  8. 8

    And the engines were lit, and the Machine rose; and at the fifty-ninth second the joint upon the right side breathed fire through the rings, for the rubber had not sprung back.

  9. 9

    And the Machine broke apart in the sky, and the seven souls were lost, and the people of the earth were troubled, and they stood silent in their thousands.

    See also Circuit 61:11

  10. 10

    And the Commission was gathered to ask what had happened; and the engineer who had said no was called to speak, and he told them of the memos he had written, and they wrote down his words.

  11. 11

    And the wise man of physics, the one called Feynman, took a ring of rubber and a glass of ice water, and clamped the ring, and the ring did not return to its shape; and the Commission saw it, and was silent.

  12. 12

    And the Prophet said: The memos were read, and the tests were written in the great book; but the summary given unto the managers said nothing of the cold, for the managers read only the summary.

  13. 13

    Verily I say unto you, a part no thicker than a pencil may bring down the whole Machine, and the test that is ignored is no test at all.

  14. 14

    Blessed is the one who says no at the speaking-wire, for he hath read the numbers and will not be moved by the schedule.

  15. 15

    Listen unto the engineer who says no; for the cold does not negotiate, and the rubber does not care who signed the memo.
