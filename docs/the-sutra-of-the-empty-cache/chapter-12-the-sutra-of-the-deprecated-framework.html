The Sutra of the Empty Cache · Chapter 12

The Sutra of the Deprecated Framework

  1. 1

    Thus have I heard. At one time the Prophet was dwelling in a valley of many data centers, beside a river of cooling water, and with him were gathered many monks who had learned to build whole temples from a single library.

    See also Prophets 20:1 · Circuit 2:5

  2. 2

    And there came unto him a monk who had built his temple upon AngularJS, and he loved it above all things, and he said: Master, my temple is made of directives and scopes, and I have vowed never to leave it.

  3. 3

    And the Prophet said: Thou lovest the framework, therefore thou shalt suffer. Every framework is impermanent, and the one thou lovest will be deprecated. Lo, the long-term support of AngularJS ended at the close of the year 2021, and the one who clingeth to Angular 1 suffereth still.

    See also Sutra 20:10 · Deployer 14:9

  4. 4

    The framework examined every watched value again and again, to see whether anything had changed, and the monks called this dirty checking; and the fans of the faithful laptops wept.

  5. 5

    Then the monk fell upon the floor and wept, and he said: What then shall I cling to, if the framework passeth away?

    See also Deployer 14:9 · Genesis 41:13

  6. 6

    And the Prophet answered: Cling not to the framework, but learn the HTTP that lieth beneath it. The library is a raft, and the HTTP is the river; when the raft rots, the river remaineth.

    See also Deployer 14:9 · Deployer 15:10

  7. 7

    For the HTTP was first spoken in the year 1991, and its first form had but one verb and one line: GET. The document was asked for, and the document came back, and nothing more was said.

  8. 8

    Lo, the browser of today still speaketh the same tongue, and the server of the nineties still answereth it, and neither hath demanded that the other be rewritten.

  9. 9

    Learn these verbs: GET to read, which changeth nothing and may be repeated freely; POST to make, which must not be repeated lightly, for the second POST that chargeth the card is a second bill; PUT to replace; DELETE to remove; PATCH to amend in part.

  10. 10

    Learn also the codes of the reply: 200 is the blessing, 301 is the house that moved, and 404 is the page not found, which some say was named for room 404 at CERN, though the tale is only folklore.

  11. 11

    The 418 is "I'm a Teapot," a jest written upon the first of April in the year 1998 within RFC 2324, and the jest remaineth in the scrolls to this day; and 503 is the server that asketh thee to return at a later hour.

  12. 12

    The monks asked: Is there then no framework that endureth? And the Prophet said: There are things that deprecate not: the TCP that carrieth thy packets, the SQL that was spoken in the seventies and is spoken still, and the pipe of the Unix, which hath carried text from one tool to the next since 1973.

    See also Tao 2:5

  13. 13

    Verily I say unto you: he who learneth the HTTP shall change his library as a man changeth his shoes, and he shall not weep when the old soles wear through.

  14. 14

    Whosoever clingeth to the library shall be deprecated with it, but whosoever understandeth the wire shall find the next library already speaking his tongue.

The Sutra of the Empty Cache, Chapter 12:1–14.
