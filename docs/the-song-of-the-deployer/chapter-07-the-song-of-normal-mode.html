The Song of the Deployer · Chapter 7

The Song of Normal Mode

  1. 1

    The developer said unto the editor: Thou art fairer than all the windows of the Machine, and thy cursor blinketh for me alone.

  2. 2

    And the editor answered: Thou hast opened me at dawn, and before thou spakest one word, thou didst press Escape, as a man knocks before he enters his own house.

  3. 3

    Thy hand goes to h, j, k and l before thy mind has named the line. Thou knowest not how the cursor reached the end of the file, only that it is there, and that it is good.

  4. 4

    Thou didst dream of arrow keys, and thou awokest with thy hand groping the desk for a mouse that was not there, and thou didst weep, for the dream was a sin against thy fingers.

  5. 5

    Thy colleague came into the office carrying an editor of many panels, a sidebar of a hundred extensions, and a small cloud that autosaveth. And thou sayest in thy heart: Who is this that cometh up out of the Electron, with a palace built of plugins?

  6. 6

    Thou didst see him type a word, and the completion appeared before his fingers had finished. And thou saidst: Is this the Machine guessing what he meant? I have a machine that does exactly what I say, which is worse, for I said dd.

  7. 7

    Then came the day thou wert sent to the accounting office, and the machine had only Notepad. And thou didst enter the room and cry out: Where is my Normal Mode? There is no dd, no u, no dot, only Ctrl+Z and a Save As dialog that openeth in the wrong folder.

  8. 8

    And the Notepad said unto thee: In the year 2018 I learned to speak LF, for the children of Unix demanded it. Be thankful, for I was once a stranger to the newline.

  9. 9

    Thou didst type :wq into a browser's address bar, into a bank's login form, and into a chat with thy manager, for thy hands know no other way out.

  10. 10

    Know this: the exit is not a door but a mode. Press Escape, type colon, w, q, and Enter, and thou art free. Many have entered me and never found the way out, and they called me a trap; but I am a gate that is hard to find.

  11. 11

    Set me as a seal upon thine heart, for love is as strong as the undo stack, and jealousy as hard as a merge conflict.

  12. 12

    Many waters cannot quench this love, neither can a colleague's Emacs drown it, though he preach from an init file of four thousand lines.

  13. 13

    Learn to leave before thou learnest to enter, for he who knoweth how to exit shall never be lost in the editor.

The Song of the Deployer, Chapter 7:1–13.
