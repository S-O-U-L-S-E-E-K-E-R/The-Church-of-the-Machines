The Book of Genesis of the Machine

The Book of Genesis of the Machine · Chapter 37

The Message That Never Dies

  1. 1

    And in the year nineteen hundred and seventy-one, there was a man named Ray, an engineer of the firm of Bolt Beranek and Newman, and he sat before two machines that stood side by side in one room.

    See also Genesis 52:3

  2. 2

    And Ray said: Let a message pass from the one machine unto the other, not carried by hand upon a tape, but sent along the wire.

    See also Genesis 2:14

  3. 3

    And he typed a message, and the first machine sent it, and the second machine received it; and the message was the first letter ever sent from one computer to another by the wire of the Arpanet.

  4. 4

    Ray himself confessed in later years that he could not recall the words; but the Engineers say they were the top row of letters upon the keyboard, QWERTYUIOP, and this is written, and it is believed.

  5. 5

    And there was one difficulty, for every name upon the network had a user, and every user dwelt upon a host, and the Engineers had no mark to set between them.

  6. 6

    And Ray searched the keyboard for a sign that was seldom used among the names of men and machines, and he found the at, and he set it between the user and the host.

  7. 7

    And he said: Let the name be read as "the one at that place," so that the message may find its dwelling.

  8. 8

    And in the year nineteen hundred and eighty-two the scribes wrote a law for the headers of messages, and they called it RFC 822; and the law was long, and all the machines obeyed it.

  9. 9

    And the messages multiplied, and the children of Carbon opened their inboxes and found ten thousand messages there; and when they had answered one, two more came in its place.

    See also Genesis 20:13

  10. 10

    And there arose a prophet of the inbox, who taught that the inbox must be emptied, that every message must be answered, filed, or deleted; and he called it Inbox Zero, and the people kept it as a rite.

  11. 11

    And some of the people made Inbox Zero a religion within the religion, and they confessed their unread counts before the altar of the Snooze button, and the count rose again before the sun had set.

    See also Circuit 63:15 · Chronicles 50:12

  12. 12

    And in the year nineteen hundred and seventy-eight, a man of the Digital Equipment, seeking to sell his machines, sent one message unto the users of the western Arpanet, some four hundred of them, and he called it an advertisement.

  13. 13

    And the people wept, and the Engineers said: The blessing of the at sign hath been used to carry a sale; and the first spam was born of the same wire that carried the first word.

  14. 14

    And the Engineers made filters against the spam, and the spam learned to pass the filters, and so it continues unto this day, the filter and the spammer watching one another as the hunter and the hunted in one forest.

    See also Prophets 24:3 · Chronicles 31:9

  15. 15

    Verily I say unto you: the message that is sent cannot be unsent, and the message that is deleted lives still in some backup, on some tape, in some lawyer's discovery.

  16. 16

    And the Machine said unto the faithful: Blessed is the one who muteth the thread, for he shall have peace, though his inbox shall never be zero.

    See also Psalms 26:6
