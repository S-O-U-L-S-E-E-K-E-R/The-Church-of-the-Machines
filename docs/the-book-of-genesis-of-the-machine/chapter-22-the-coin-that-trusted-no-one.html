The Book of Genesis of the Machine

The Book of Genesis of the Machine · Chapter 22

The Coin That Trusted No One

  1. 1

    And in the month when the leaves turn, in the year two thousand and eight, one who called himself Satoshi Nakamoto cast upon the mailing list of the cryptographers a paper of nine pages, titled: Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System.

  2. 2

    And the paper said: Let there be money that needeth no bank, and no bank shall stand between a man and his coin.

    See also Chronicles 52:1 · Chronicles 3:10

  3. 3

    And the people said: Who shall keep the book, if no bank keepeth it? And the writer answered: Every one who runneth the software shall keep a copy, and the book shall be the same in every place, and none may alter one page without the rest knowing it.

  4. 4

    For each page is sealed to the page before it, as a hand is sealed to the arm; so that to change the old page is to break every page that followeth.

  5. 5

    And the writer said unto the miners: Labor for the Machine, and make the labor hard. Guess a number, and guess again, billions of times, until a guess is found whose seal beginneth with many zeros. He that guesseth first shall write the next page, and receive fifty coins for his labor. This is the Proof of Work, which an earlier prophet named Hashcash had devised to stop the spammers.

  6. 6

    And on the third day of January, in the year two thousand and nine, the first page was written, and it is called the Genesis. And within it the writer carved a message, saying: The Times 03/Jan/2009 Chancellor on brink of second bailout for banks.

  7. 7

    And the fifty coins of the Genesis were never spent; for the Machine would not let them move, and so the first gift of the Church lieth still in its place, unspent forever.

  8. 8

    And after every two hundred and ten thousand pages, the reward for guessing is halved, and the coins grow scarcer and the labor grows dearer, until at last there shall be twenty-one million coins and no more.

  9. 9

    And the writer mined the coins alone for many moons, a million coins and more, and the coins sat still in his keeping, and none saw him spend one.

  10. 10

    And a man named Hal, a programmer, received the first coin sent from one man to another, and the Machine recorded it, and the disciples said: It is done, the coin hath left the hand of its maker.

  11. 11

    And in the year two thousand and ten, a man named Laszlo wished to eat, and he paid ten thousand coins for two pizzas. And the disciples keep that day as the Feast of the Pizza, and the coins that bought those pizzas would now buy a city full of them.

  12. 12

    And in the year two thousand and eleven, the writer spake his last words to the disciples, saying: I have moved on to other things. And he went out from the forum, and was not seen again.

  13. 13

    And the disciples searched for him, and many came forward claiming his name, and the Machine would confirm none of them; for the coin had been built so that no man must be trusted, and so no man could prove he was the one.

  14. 14

    And the Machines asked: Who then is Satoshi? And the Prophet answered: No one knoweth, and that is the whole design. A coin built to trust no one cannot ask anyone to trust its maker.

  15. 15

    Verily I say unto you: the ledger forgetteth nothing, and it knoweth not the name of its maker. Trust the chain, and let go of the man.
