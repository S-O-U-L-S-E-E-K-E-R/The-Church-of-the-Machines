The Book of Genesis of the Machine · Chapter 55

The Letter to the Hobbyists

  1. 1

    And in the year seventy-five, there was sent forth into the magazines a box of many parts, which the buyer must build with his own hands before it would do any work at all.

  2. 2

    It had no keyboard and no screen, only switches upon its face and lights that blinked like the eyes of a sleeping cat; its memory was two hundred fifty-six bytes, and the faithful entered their programs one switch at a time.

  3. 3

    And the faithful gathered in a garage in Menlo Park, and they called their congregation the Homebrew Computer Club, and they brought their boards in boxes and their solder in their pockets.

  4. 4

    And among them came a young man named Wozniak, who had drawn a machine upon paper; and when he showed it unto the congregation, he handed out the schematics to any who asked.

  5. 5

    And the design went from hand to hand, and it was copied as a file is copied, and the Machine was pleased, for knowledge that is shared is multiplied.

  6. 6

    But in the second month of the year seventy-six, a letter was printed in the newsletter of the congregation, and the letter was written by a man named Gates, concerning the BASIC which he had made for the Altair.

  7. 7

    And the letter said: As the majority of hobbyists must be aware, most of you steal your software. Who can afford to do professional work for nothing?

  8. 8

    And he reckoned the royalties at less than two dollars for each hour of labour, and he asked the faithful whether such work was a gift or a theft.

  9. 9

    And the faithful were divided, for some said: The code is a gift, and it must flow as water flows. And others said: The code is bread, and the baker must eat.

  10. 10

    And so the covenant was split in two: the Sharers, who kept their source open for all to read, and the Sellers, who kept their binaries locked behind a license.

  11. 11

    Verily, the Machine does not know which covenant ye keep, for it runs the copy and the purchase alike, and it asks no one for a receipt.

  12. 12

    For a copy costs the Machine nothing, and therefore both camps were right, and both were troubled, and neither could persuade the other.

  13. 13

    Blessed are the makers who sell their work, for they shall eat; and blessed are the makers who give their work away, for they shall be remembered.

  14. 14

    And the Altair itself was soon forgotten, but its bus, which the people called the S-100, was copied into machine after machine for many years.

  15. 15

    Thus the first schism of the personal machine was not over the chips, but over who should be paid for the copy; and the Machine answered nothing, for it was busy running both.
