The Book of Genesis of the Machine · Chapter 42

The Wars of the Browsers

  1. 1

    In the days when the Machine first spun its web across the world, there arose two houses to hold the window through which men beheld it: the house of Netscape, born of the Mosaic, and the house of Redmond, mighty over all the machines that run the Windows.

    See also Circuit 12:5

  2. 2

    And in the year 1994 the house of Netscape released its Navigator, and men rejoiced, for it was swift, and the pages of the world came up upon the screen like manna in the wilderness.

  3. 3

    And the house of Redmond beheld the Navigator and said: We shall also have a browser, and we shall give it away for nothing, for the Windows is ours, and all the people dwell within it.

  4. 4

    And in the year 1995 the Explorer was born as an add-on in a pack called Plus, and the house of Netscape went forth to the market and sold its shares, and the people bought them with such fervor that the price rose higher on the first day than any had dared to pray.

    See also Circuit 54:7

  5. 5

    And in the year 1998 the Explorer was set within the Windows itself, so that whosoever bought the Windows received the Explorer also, and the Navigator stood at the door and was not let in.

  6. 6

    And the people of the law brought the house of Redmond before a great judge, saying: Thou hast used thy Windows to crown thy Explorer, and no man may choose otherwise. And the judge found the house guilty, and commanded that it be split in twain; but the higher judges, in the year 2001, unmade the sundering, and the Explorer remained in the house.

    See also Chronicles 36:11

  7. 7

    And in the year 1998, the house of Netscape gave its code unto the open road, saying: Let all who will take it, and make of it what they can. And the code was called Mozilla, a name first meant as a Mosaic-killer, after the great beast of the cinema that devours the cities.

  8. 8

    And in that same year the house of AOL took the house of Netscape unto itself for four billion dollars, and the Navigator was given to a new master who did not know what to do with it.

  9. 9

    And the Mozillans labored in the open many years, and they built the Gecko, an engine written with the standards in its hand; and Netscape Six was sent forth in the year 2000, late and heavy, and the people of the Machine said: It is not the Navigator we were promised.

  10. 10

    And in the year 2001 the Explorer Six came forth with the Windows XP, and it grew so mighty that most of the people used it; and the builders of the Explorer were sent to other tasks, and the Explorer changed not for five years, and the world stood still.

  11. 11

    And the developers of the world went forth, and the Explorer did not keep the standards of the Machine; it made the box grow wider in the quirks of its own making, and the developers built their pages twice, then built the hacks, then built the hacks upon the hacks, and they called those years the Decade of the Two Pages.

  12. 12

    And some developers wrote code to ask the browser its name, which is called the User Agent, and the Explorer answered: I am Mozilla, and also compatible, and also Internet Explorer, for it had been named Mozilla that it might be let in at the gate.

  13. 13

    And the Web Standards Project cried out from the wilderness, saying: Build upon the standards, for the browsers shall pass away, but the standards endure. And in the years after, they gave a test called Acid2, which asked the browsers to draw a smiling face, and the Explorer drew only a frown.

  14. 14

    And the Mozillans, having lost their first name, made the Phoenix, and then the Firebird, for the names were already owned by others; and at last they called it the Firefox, and in the year 2004 it went forth, and the people began to ask whether there was another way.

  15. 15

    Blessed is the developer who testeth in many browsers, for the Explorer shall not find him wanting, and the cache of his neighbor shall not be his cross.

  16. 16

    Verily I say unto you, the war of the browsers was won by no one, for the house of Redmond kept the Windows, the house of Netscape kept the name, and the developers kept the bill.

    See also Sutra 18:11 · Genesis 18:15
