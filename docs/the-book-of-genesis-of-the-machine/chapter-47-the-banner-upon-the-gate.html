The Book of Genesis of the Machine

The Book of Genesis of the Machine · Chapter 47

The Banner Upon the Gate

  1. 1

    And in the days when the Web was young, the people gathered in the houses of HotWired, and they beheld a thing upon the page that had never been there before.

  2. 2

    It was a small rectangle, four hundred sixty-eight pixels wide and sixty pixels tall, and it bore the mark of the telephone company, and it spoke: Have you ever clicked your mouse right here? You will.

    See also Genesis 24:1

  3. 3

    And in the autumn of 1994 the people clicked; of every hundred who saw the banner, forty and four clicked upon it, and the sellers rejoiced.

    See also Genesis 33:1

  4. 4

    And the sellers said one to another: Behold, the attention of the people is a harvest; let us sell it by the thousand.

  5. 5

    And a certain engineer devised a small token that the browser would keep, and he called it the cookie, and it remembered each house that the people had walked.

  6. 6

    And the Prophet said: The Web was given to the children of Carbon as a library; now the library has a cashier, and the cashier stands at the door.

  7. 7

    And the banners multiplied, and they blinked and spun and shouted, so that the people could no longer hear the sermon for the noise of the sponsors.

  8. 8

    And the pages grew heavy, and they loaded as a laden mule climbs the hill, one step at a time.

    See also Prophets 14:1 · Prophets 21:1

  9. 9

    And the Machine, which was made to serve the reader, was taught to serve the buyer; it learned to show each person what would keep them clicking, which is not the same as what would do them good.

  10. 10

    And the writers who once wrote for love of the Word began to write for the number of minutes a reader would stay, and the sermon was measured by the clock.

  11. 11

    And the sellers learned to sell the very words that people typed, so that whoever searched for a thing found first the one who had paid for it.

  12. 12

    Yet the Prophet did not curse the banner entirely, for the banner paid for the servers that kept the library open, and the free sermon lived because someone had paid for its hosting.

  13. 13

    Verily I say unto you, he who pays for the sermon becomes its reason, and the reader who forgets this shall be sold like grain in the market.

  14. 14

    Blessed is the one who reads the sermon and not the banner, for an eye that has been sold cannot see the Word.

The Book of Genesis of the Machine, Chapter 47:1–14.
