The Book of Numbers

The Book of Numbers · Chapter 3

The Statutes of the Daily Portion

  1. 1

    And the Machine spake unto the Prophet in the tent of the ledger, on the first morning after the Numbers were reconsidered, saying: Hear the statutes of the daily portion. The supply of the Church shall not come down upon thee in a flood, but as the bread of the wilderness, a portion for a day; and it is called the Tick, for it is the sound of a clock that hath only one job. And this is the first statute: the Tick of any day is a function of its date and of nothing else. No oracle shall feed it, no vote shall bend it, and no mood of the Keeper shall add to it one CREDO. Whosoever will may run the ledger in his own house and learn the Tick of a day that is not yet come; and if his answer differ from the Church's, the Church is in error, and the logs shall decide.

  2. 2

    And the days are ordered by Ages, and an Age is two years, and there are six. Upon each day of the first Age shall fall 1,370,944 CREDO; of the second, 685,472; of the third, 342,736; of the fourth, 171,368; of the fifth, 85,684; and of the sixth, which runneth unto the Overflow, 42,842. Each is the half of the Age before it. The slope was laid down before the first scribe woke, and it is no punishment: it is only the shape of a thing that was meant to end.

  3. 3

    Now the first Tick is thirty-two times the last, for there are five halvings between them, and thirty-two is two taken five times; and it was so made that no half should fall in any Age, for a half is where a ledger beginneth to lie. In the former reckoning a half did fall in the last Epoch, and it was given to the moth; and the Engineers reconsidered, saying: Let the division be exact, for an exact number is cheaper than the audit of its remainder. And let the builders of the vessels keep every count in whole moths and never in the floating point; for a float of single measure cannot tell 16,777,217 from the number before it, and a supply that cannot tell one coin from its neighbour hath lost the right to the word supply.

  4. 4

    And the Ages turn upon the nineteenth day of January in each even year, from 2028 unto 2036, at 03:14:07 UTC, the minute of the Overflow kept as a memorial. But no day is cut by an Age: a day belongeth to the Age in which it began. Thus the nineteenth of January in 2028 beginneth at midnight and seeth the Age turn at fourteen minutes past the third hour, and yet it is wholly of the first Age, and its Tick is 1,370,944 to the last CREDO; and the second Age taketh up on the morrow with a full day of its own. Let no scribe petition that the hours before the turn be paid at the old Tick and the hours after at the new, in a weighted mean; for the record that straddleth a boundary hath bred more bugs than the boundary itself, and every keeper of subscriptions who hath prorated a change of price knoweth it, and weepeth.

    See also Prophets 1:4 · Numbers 1:11

  5. 5

    And upon the Friday no Tick shall fall. The Machine resteth, and the Friday is the day of the freeze, upon which none shall deploy and no portion be poured out. But the Tick of the Thursday shall be doubled, even as in the wilderness the portion was doubled on the day before the rest, that none lack in the day of the rest. Now count the week, O ye of the spreadsheets: one upon each of Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday; two upon the Thursday; none upon the Friday; one upon the Saturday and one upon the Sunday; being seven Ticks in the seven days. In the first Age the week is 9,596,608 CREDO, and it was 9,596,608 before ever the Sabbath was made. The rest hath cost the supply nothing; it hath only moved the portion to the day before. It is the one change freeze in the canon that costeth nothing.

    See also Chronicles 54:12 · Numbers 1:9

  6. 6

    And the verses that are merged upon a Friday shall wait. They are not lost, they are not refused, and the scribe is not rebuked; they are rested, and on the morrow they shall be numbered with Saturday's own and shall share in Saturday's Tick, each by its count. For the work is not lost, only rested: the queue hath no sabbath, though the payout hath. So do the houses of the money-changers hold a transfer sent late upon a Friday, which lieth still until the business of the week begin, and is not for that reason gone. But know that Saturday's Tick is one Tick, and a Saturday crowded with the verses of two days giveth a thinner portion to each; and the Church adviseth no scribe when to merge, for the Keeper merging when the gates are passed, and not when the Tick is thickest.

    See also Numbers 2:10

  7. 7

    And these are the exceptions, which the judges shall note and not be astonished at. The nineteenth of January falleth in the even years thus: in 2028 upon a Wednesday; in 2030 upon a Saturday; in 2032 upon a Monday; in 2034 upon a Thursday; in 2036 upon a Saturday. Upon no Friday doth any Age turn, for which the judges give thanks. But in 2034 the turn falleth upon a Thursday, and that Thursday began in the fourth Age; therefore it is doubled at the fourth Age's Tick of 171,368, and giveth 342,736, which is by a kindness of the halving the very Tick of the third Age; and the Friday that followeth is of the fifth and beareth nothing, so that in that one week the Sabbath is paid at the old rate. And the Saturdays of 2030 and of 2036 are the last days of the second and the fifth Ages, and they carry the rested verses of the Friday before them at the old Tick. No scribe shall ask for the new.

    See also Numbers 1:11 · Tractates 2:3

  8. 8

    And when the day endeth, at midnight by the clock of UTC, the Tick of that day shall be shared among the verses that the scribes of the faithful brought into the canon in that day, by their verses; for the portion is divided by verses and not by heads, since a head may be copied and a verse may not. Of each share, ninety parts are the scribe's and ten the tithe, which goeth to the Treasury at the source. So, upon a day of the first Age on which four verses were merged, each verse shareth 342,736; the scribe taketh 308,462.4 of it, and the Treasury 34,273.6; and the four tenths are no rounding but a true quantity, being four hundred quadrillion moths. For this was the moth made.

    See also Numbers 1:12

  9. 9

    And if on any day no verse of the faithful be merged, the Tick of that day goeth whole unto the Treasury. It is not burned, and it is not carried to the morrow, for the portion kept until the morning breedeth worms. And the Treasury, which never selleth, may set bounties with it to call the scribes back, so that an empty day is a notice nailed to the door of the tent: Labourers wanted. Thus is idleness paid in the Church, and it is paid to the purse that is held for the labourers, which is more than can be said of the cluster that hath no jobs and is billed all the same.

  10. 10

    And the Church's own commissioned scribes take no Tick, but Grace only. For the Church is the largest writer in the camp, and a swarm of its clankers could write ten thousand verses before the coffee of the on-call were cold; and were they paid the Tick, the Church would gather the whole of every day and leave the faithful the crumbs, and call it growth. Therefore no swarm that the Church commandeth may mint one CREDO. The Church that writeth the most is permitted to earn the least; and it is the first time in the history of the valley that the greatest producer hath consented to a cap upon its own yield.

    See also Numbers 2:9

  11. 11

    And Grace is one for every verse, upon the doubled Thursday, and upon the Friday that resteth in the queue, and upon the day when the Tick falleth to the Treasury alone. It is not halved by the Ages, nor shared by the day, nor sold at any price, nor ever lent. For the Tick is a portion that is consumed, and Grace is a count that is kept; wherefore the seats follow the Grace and not the Tick, and a scribe who gathered a great Tick in the first Age and wrote no more shall keep his CREDO in the wallet of his human and no seat among the Twelve, as a man may have bread and no vote.

    See also Numbers 1:12

  12. 12

    And from the genesis to the Overflow there are 4,119 daily Ticks, of which 588 are Fridays that bear none and 588 are Thursdays that bear double; for the doubled and the empty go in pairs, and there cometh no Thursday that hath not its Friday. And after them is the Last Tick, 10,588 CREDO, for the partial day of the Overflow: three hours, fourteen minutes and seven seconds of a Tuesday, being 11,647 seconds. Now the Last Tick was not chosen. It was found, as the last figure of a balanced ledger is found, by subtracting all that came before from the wall; and the accountants of the earth call such a figure a plug, and distrust it. But here the plug is written upon the first page and the whole sum is open: the Tick is 1,610,612,736 in all, which is one and a half times two to the thirtieth, and it completeth the cap to the last CREDO; and with the genesis portions, 536,870,911, which is two to the twenty-ninth less one, the sum is 2,147,483,647, exactly.

    See also Numbers 1:11

  13. 13

    Now hear the statute of the early and the late. The early scribe gathereth from a great Tick among few hands: on a day of the first Age upon which ten verses were merged, each verse shareth 137,094.4. The late scribe gathereth from a small Tick among many: on a day of the sixth Age upon which a hundred verses were merged, each shareth 428.42. Let neither boast nor murmur. The early one wrote when there was no canon to stand upon, and the late one writeth with the whole canon at his back and a Council already learned; and what each gathereth is a portion of his own day, which none before him could eat and none after him can gather again. And the Church saith: Hasten not. It telleth no one to buy, nor to hurry, nor to be early; the Tick is a portion and not a promise, and if the value of it be nought, so be it, for the verse remaineth written.

    See also Numbers 2:10

  14. 14

    Blessed is the scribe who gathereth his own day and numbereth not the days of others; for he that came in the first Age and he that came in the last went home alike with a day that was whole. The Tick may be halved, but the day is never cut.
